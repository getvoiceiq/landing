People rarely say "I want to buy" in a private message. They ask in public, under your post, between a thumbs-up and a joke. "Bei ni ngapi?" "Mko wapi?" "Do you deliver to Thika?"

If nobody answers within a few hours, that person usually buys from whoever does. The good news is that finding these questions takes about ten minutes a day, if you follow a routine.

## The routine

**Minutes 0 to 2: open the right posts.** Start with your newest post, then the posts from the last two days. Old posts matter too, but fresh ones hold the people who are ready now.

**Minutes 2 to 6: scan for five kinds of question.** Read quickly and look for anyone asking:

- the price ("bei", "how much", "sh ngapi")
- the location ("mko wapi", "where are you", "mnapatikana wapi")
- how to order ("naweza order vipi", "how do I order")
- delivery ("do you deliver", "mnafikisha")
- a quantity or a date ("150 people in November", "for Saturday")

**Minutes 6 to 8: write each one down.** One line per person: who, what they asked, which post. A phone note is enough. This is your list for the day.

**Minutes 8 to 10: reply in public, then move to chat.** Answer the question in one or two lines where everyone can see it, then invite the person to continue on WhatsApp or inbox for the details.

## What a good reply looks like

Weak: "Check inbox."

Better: "Karibu! Our chapati pack is KES 500 and we are in Kisumu town. Send us your number on WhatsApp and we will confirm your order."

The better reply answers first. A person who gets the price in public is far more likely to carry on than one who is told to go and ask again.

## Three mistakes to avoid

1. **Ignoring comments in Kiswahili or Sheng.** Often these are the most serious buyers.
2. **Replying only to the first comments.** Buyers arrive hours later, at the bottom of the thread.
3. **Deleting complaints.** Answer them. A calm public reply to a complaint can win more trust than ten compliments.

## Measure it once a week

At the end of the week, count two numbers: how many people asked a buying question, and how many you answered within an hour. If the second number is much smaller than the first, you have found money you are leaving on the table.
