title: Five complaints to count every week, and what to do about each

---

Reading a few angry comments feels bad but tells you little. Counting them tells you a lot. When you know that 21 people mentioned slow delivery and 3 mentioned price, you know where to spend your week.

Here are five complaint types that appear in almost every business's comments. Count each one weekly, even with a simple tally on paper.

## 1. Slow delivery or slow service

**What it sounds like:** "Delivery took 2 hours." "Nimeagiza jana bado sijapata."

**First fix:** Tell people the real time before they order. A promise you keep beats a faster one you break. Then look at what causes the delay: the kitchen, the rider, or the dispatch.

## 2. Wrong or incomplete orders

**What it sounds like:** "This is the third time you got my order wrong."

**First fix:** Read the order back before it leaves. One extra check costs a minute and saves a refund and a public complaint.

## 3. Prices that changed without warning

**What it sounds like:** "Why did the price go up again?"

**First fix:** Post price changes before they happen, with a one-line reason. People forgive a price rise they were warned about.

## 4. Rude or careless staff

**What it sounds like:** "Staff were so rude at the counter."

**First fix:** Do not argue in public. Apologise, ask for details in a private message, and speak to the team. If the same name or branch keeps coming up, you have found the real problem.

## 5. No reply

**What it sounds like:** "Nobody replies to inbox." "Mnaonaje, mbona hamjibu?"

**First fix:** Choose one person and one time of day for replies. Many "no reply" complaints are really "no reply within an hour" complaints, and an hour is achievable.

## How to count

At the end of each week, go through the week's comments and put a mark next to the matching complaint. Write the totals in one place and compare them to last week. After a month you will see which problems are shrinking and which are growing.
